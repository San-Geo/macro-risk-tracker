**WHO THIS HITS:** *Anyone with savings, a mortgage, or a pension — this sits under the US Treasury market, which sets the price of almost all borrowing and most investments.*

**CURRENT THREAT LEVEL:** *Leverage high, volatility elevated*

**Since the last edition:** Two key indicators moved into a higher band between late June and early November 2026. Treasury volatility, as measured by the MOVE index, climbed from a calm 66.79 at the last edition to above 100 by late October — the MOVE index rose significantly in this period, decoupling from equity volatility in an unusual pattern, quite different from the March spike that hit both markets simultaneously. Separately, the 10-year Treasury yield moved above 5% for the first time since 2023 in mid-September and briefly touched 5.34% on 1 October, its highest since 2002. The SOFR–EFFR spread also widened, from 0.03% to 0.07% as of 30 October, signalling mild additional strain in repo funding conditions. On the structural side, the SEC redrawn a part of the Treasury repo clearing perimeter on 30 October, issuing final exemptive relief for a capped amount of non-US client repo and expanding the inter-affiliate exclusion — the wider clearing mandate remains intact, but its cross-border edge is now less blunt. Together these shifts pushed the threat level from Elevated (6/10) to High (7/10).

**Why this level:** Among the largest hidden-leverage points in markets; Treasury volatility has now climbed to a level historically associated with stress episodes, repo funding costs are edging higher, and central clearing — the main structural fix — is still weeks away for cash and months away for repo. **Raises it:** A further spike in Treasury volatility or repo rates forcing simultaneous margin calls across leveraged funds; the clearing carve-out weakening the mandate's scope; ongoing yield pressure from record debt issuance. **Lowers it:** Central clearing implemented on schedule at end-2026; Treasury volatility retreating to the mid-60s range seen in early 2026; a further voluntary reduction in basis-trade leverage.

### What is happening

**\[Established\]** A small group of highly leveraged hedge funds has become a central marginal player in the $30tn US Treasury market through the cash-futures basis trade — buying cash Treasuries, shorting Treasury futures, and financing the position in the overnight repo market. As of early 2026, hedge funds held around $2 trillion in Treasuries overall, representing a record 7% of the entire Treasury market.

**\[Contested\]** Estimates of the trade's size have shifted since the last edition. By mid-2026, the total leverage associated with the trade had swelled to around $1 trillion, before hedge funds pulled back more than $200 billion in positions in subsequent months. Assets tied to leveraged basis strategies fell about 20% in 2026 to roughly $1.2 trillion, as higher rates, tighter price differences and changing market conditions reduced potential returns. **\[Established\]** What is not disputed: leverage is still concentrated and high. The Federal Reserve's Financial Stability Report released in May explicitly noted that hedge-fund leverage remains near historical highs and is highly concentrated among large funds, warning that if fund financing channels are suddenly disrupted, high leverage could trigger risk spillovers.

**\[Established\]** The SEC's mandate to centrally clear cash Treasuries and Treasury repo remains the regulators' main structural response. The compliance date for cash Treasury transactions is approaching on 31 December 2026, with repo transactions following on 30 June 2027; daily cleared Treasury volumes are already approximately 165% higher than before the Commission's proposal. **\[Single-source\]** On 30 October 2026, just days before this edition's data date, the SEC issued final exemptive relief allowing a capped amount of otherwise in-scope non-US client repo to remain bilateral and expanding the inter-affiliate exclusion — the wider clearing mandate remains intact, but its cross-border edge is now less blunt. The compliance date for cash Treasury transactions was not moved; the SEC does not currently intend to extend these deadlines.

**\[Established\]** MOVE index readings above 100 are the level at which basis-trade margin and haircut pressures historically become self-reinforcing. The MOVE index climbed sharply in October 2026, above 110 at its peak, while the VIX stayed near 15–16, an unusual decoupling.

### Why it is under-covered

It is pure market plumbing with no retail hook, no villain, and a name that signals "technical." Yet it sits beneath the asset that prices everything else on Earth. When it breaks, it does so violently and the public only learns the term afterwards. The bond-volatility surge of October 2026 received far less mainstream attention than concurrent equity moves, even as MOVE outpaced VIX by a historically unusual margin.

### The debate

**Regulators argue:** the trade is large, concentrated, funded by ultra-short repo, and exposed to a doom loop — a volatility spike raises futures margins and repo haircuts at once, forcing funds to dump cash Treasuries into a falling market, widening the very basis they were arbitraging. Regulators warn that hedge funds' heavy leverage — particularly in Treasury cash-futures basis trades, where leverage can exceed 20× — could trigger forced deleveraging and liquidity crises under market stress.

**The industry argues:** the trade is functionally useful — it keeps futures and cash prices aligned, adds auction demand and liquidity in normal times — and concentration is declining as spreads compress. There is currently no clear evidence of a broader deterioration in hedge-fund demand for Treasuries or systemic stress across the basis trade, according to some bank strategists. Advocates note that hedge funds' willingness to trade rather than simply hold bonds to maturity can provide two-sided liquidity during both rallies and sell-offs, which could ultimately stabilise rate moves and reduce volatility.

**Both sides accept:** the position is genuinely large, genuinely concentrated in a handful of funds, central to Treasury functioning, and that central clearing is imminent. The disagreement is only about how it behaves under acute stress.

### Stress test (the benign case)

The optimistic read has two planks. First, hedge funds have scaled back Treasury basis positions as shrinking price discrepancies have made the strategy less attractive, with notional value falling to about $900bn from approximately $1.26tn at the start of 2026 — a smaller position is a smaller potential disorderly unwind. Second, the industry has made significant strides in preparation for the implementation dates, extended to 31 December 2026 for cash Treasury clearing and 30 June 2027 for Treasury repo, meaning the structural fix is now weeks rather than years away. The weakness in that case: MOVE above 100 is precisely the zone where margin and haircut pressures compound; the Fed's Standing Repo Facility remains untested at scale during a true panic; and the new repo carve-out leaves some cross-border positions outside the clearing net.

### Historical parallel

March 2020: basis-trade unwinding was a major contributor to the Treasury market dysfunction that forced the Fed into massive emergency purchases. Further back, the 1998 collapse of Long-Term Capital Management showed how a relative-value arbitrage on tiny spreads, levered enormously, can threaten the system when the spread moves the "wrong" way and convergence arrives too late to meet today's margin call. Both episodes ended with official intervention, not market self-correction. The October 2026 episode of MOVE rising above 110 while equity volatility stayed subdued rhymes with an early-warning pattern that preceded both events — bond stress that equity markets initially ignored.

::: box
**In plain terms**

A handful of hedge funds have borrowed enormous sums to run a razor-thin-margin trade in US government bonds — the safest, most important market in the world. In calm times it helps the market work. But between July and October 2026, Treasury volatility climbed sharply back above the 100-mark, the level at which these trades historically come under the most pressure. The funds have partially retreated — positions are smaller than earlier in the year — and mandatory central clearing is now weeks away for the cash market. But the system is not yet fixed, and a further lurch in yields could still force simultaneous rushed selling into a market that is already on edge.
:::

::: box
**What you can do now**

**Keep an emergency cash cushion.** If the bond market seizes up, everything wobbles for a while; cash means you aren't forced to sell at the worst moment.

**Don't assume "bonds are always safe."** In a 2020-style episode, even US Treasuries briefly fell; spreading across asset types matters more than owning any one 'safe' thing.

**Favour simple, low-cost, broad funds** over leveraged or exotic bond products you don't fully understand.

**Check the duration in your fixed-income holdings.** With long-dated yields near multi-decade highs and volatility elevated, longer-duration bonds carry more price risk than usual; understanding what you own matters.

**Mostly, this is awareness.** It's a plumbing risk you can't personally fix — but knowing why a sudden bond-market air-pocket can happen helps you avoid panic-selling into one.
:::

### What to watch

- The MOVE index: it reached above 110 in October 2026 and was still near 100 at the data date; a sustained move back above 110–120 would be a clear escalation signal for basis-trade stress.

- The SOFR–EFFR spread and repo rates at quarter-ends: the spread has already shifted from 0.03% to 0.07%; further widening would signal tightening dealer balance-sheet capacity and rising funding costs for the trade.

- SEC Treasury-clearing implementation milestones: the cash-market deadline of 31 December 2026 is the single most important near-term event; any further delay or material carve-out would leave the vulnerability structurally intact.

- Form PF and OFR repo data showing whether the voluntary deleveraging of 2026 continues or reverses, particularly in longer-duration basis positions which remain active even as shorter-tenor trades wind down.
