The most consequential shift since the last edition is the convergence of three previously separate threat streams — an energy supply shock, a sovereign debt flare-up, and a private-credit deterioration — into a single, mutually reinforcing pressure on long-end interest rates. That common driver explains why the overall tracker has moved from 6.5 to 7.8 and why four stories have changed band.

The single largest move belongs to **Story 3 (Private credit)**, which jumped from 6 to 8. The Fitch US Private Credit Default Rate more than doubled in nine months to a record 6.3%, redemption gates at semi-liquid funds became routine rather than exceptional, and the Fed's September rate hike directly raised debt-service costs for borrowers who are already stretched [Established]. That hike was itself partly a response to the oil-price shock flowing from **Story 17 (Sea lanes)**, which moved from 6 to 7: the Strait of Hormuz has remained effectively shut for Day 246 as of 2 November, pushing WTI crude to \$96.10 — a level that, as the Dallas Fed's modelling confirmed, is consistent with a closure now exceeding two quarters [Established]. **Story 1 (Treasury basis trade)** moved from 6 to 7 as the MOVE index surged past 100 and the 10-year Treasury yield briefly touched 5.34% on 1 October — its highest since 2002 — driven partly by the same energy-inflation dynamic [Established]. **Story 5 (Clearinghouses)** moved from 4 to 5: the VIX and MOVE index both rose, raising the probability of a procyclical margin episode [Established]. **Story 7 (CRE / regional banks)** moved from 5 to 6 as CMBS office delinquency crossed into a higher stress band and the pace of bank failures accelerated [Established].

The common driver threading through all five upgrades is a higher-for-longer rate environment that was not the base case six months ago: it stresses private credit borrowers (Story 3), loads the Treasury basis trade (Story 1), widens sovereign spreads in France (Story 11), and makes the CRE refinancing wall steeper (Story 7). The Gulf disruption is the upstream cause of the rate shock; the rest of the system is the transmission.
