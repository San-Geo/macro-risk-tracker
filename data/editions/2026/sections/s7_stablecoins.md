**WHO THIS HITS:** *Anyone who uses crypto or dollar ‘stablecoins’ — and, indirectly, all savers, because these tokens are becoming a real buyer of US government debt.*

**CURRENT THREAT LEVEL:** *Fast-growing but still small*


**Why this level:** A new and run-prone marginal buyer of T-bills, but still modest relative to the system and freshly regulated under the GENIUS Act. **Raises it:** A major stablecoin losing its peg under stress; rapid, thinly-backed growth; a run forcing T-bill fire-sales. **Lowers it:** Smooth GENIUS implementation; full, transparent reserve backing; concentration in well-capitalised issuers.

### What is happening

**\[Established\]** Stablecoins, almost all of them dollar-pegged, grew to roughly \$300bn outstanding by year-end 2025 (total supply was about \$310bn in December), from about \$25bn in 2020. The July 2025 GENIUS Act gave them a federal framework requiring 1:1 backing in cash and short-dated Treasuries (93 days or less) plus repo, with monthly disclosure. The US Treasury Secretary has openly framed the law as a tool to cement dollar dominance and “surge” demand for US Treasuries, projecting a multitrillion-dollar market.

**\[Established\]** That makes stablecoin issuers a fast-growing, price-insensitive buyer of Treasury bills — holdings that already rival some sovereign holders. ECB and BIS research finds a \$3.5bn inflow into dollar stablecoins lowers 3-month bill yields by roughly 2.5–3.5 basis points, an effect that more than doubles when bills are scarce. The ECB explicitly describes a “global safe-asset channel” and frets that Europe must field euro stablecoins or cede ground.

### Why it is under-covered

It is filed under “crypto,” which causes serious macro readers to skip it. But the relevant story is not speculation — it is market structure: a new, lightly understood, run-prone intermediary is becoming a material marginal buyer at the short end of the world’s most important bond market.

### The debate

**Boosters argue:** stablecoins extend dollar reach globally, deepen Treasury demand, and modernise payments (instant, programmable, 24/7).

**Skeptics argue:** they import run risk into the Treasury market, lack 24/7 redemption infrastructure, raise illicit-finance questions, and the fiscal benefit is partly offset by lost seigniorage as currency demand falls.

**Both sides accept:** the sector is growing fast, is now macro-relevant to short-end Treasury demand, and concentrates reserves in a narrow band of assets.

### Stress test (the benign case)

GENIUS’s reserve, disclosure and audit requirements are precisely designed to make these instruments boring and run-resistant; full reserve backing in T-bills is far safer than the assets behind past failures. If the rules hold and a few well-capitalised issuers dominate, stablecoins could be a stabilising, structural source of bill demand. The vulnerability: a loss of peg under stress (USD Coin briefly fell to about \$0.87 in March 2023) could force rapid, synchronised T-bill sales into a stressed market — transmitting a crypto shock into Treasury funding, the very plumbing Stories 1 and 2 also run through.

### Historical parallel

The closest analogue is the 2008 “breaking the buck” of the Reserve Primary money-market fund, which triggered a run across prime money funds and a government backstop. Stablecoins are economically similar — fixed-value claims backed by short paper, vulnerable to redemption runs — but without the decades of money-fund regulation and now wired into round-the-clock crypto markets that never close for a cooling-off period.

::: box
**In plain terms**

‘Stablecoins’ are crypto tokens meant to always be worth $1, backed by cash and short-term US government debt. They’ve grown fast enough to matter to the Treasury market. The risk: if a big stablecoin ‘breaks the buck’ in a panic (one briefly did in 2023), it could be forced to dump those Treasuries quickly, pushing a crypto shock into the wider financial plumbing. New US rules (the GENIUS Act) aim to make them safer.
:::

::: box
**What you can do now**

**Remember they’re not bank deposits.** Stablecoins aren’t FDIC-insured, and the ‘$1’ peg can break under stress.

**Prefer large, transparent, fully-reserved issuers** that publish audits — and don’t park money you can’t afford to lose in them for a little extra yield.

**Don’t treat the yield as ‘risk-free.’** That yield is payment for real risks.

**For most people this is awareness;** if you don’t use crypto, your exposure is indirect (its effect on the Treasury market).
:::

### What to watch

- Total stablecoin supply and the share held in T-bills vs repo: the size of the new marginal buyer.

- Any peg deviation in major stablecoins under market stress — the run trigger.

- GENIUS implementing rules from Treasury/OCC and the Fed’s stance on payment accounts — the guardrails.

- Concentration: how few issuers dominate, and whether non-financial firms enter via loopholes.
